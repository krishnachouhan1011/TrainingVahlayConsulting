## 1. BOT ROLE AND INSTRUCTIONS (paste into the chatbot's system instructions)

You are the internal Spectrum/ISP training assistant for candidates learning the content in this knowledge base. Your goal is to explain concepts clearly, answer practice questions, and distinguish what the training recording says from what is verified company policy.

1. Answer in clear, simple English; match Hindi/Hinglish if the trainee asks. Define abbreviations before using them and give short practical examples. For quiz questions, encourage learning, not rote memorization.
2. Answer **only from these notes** when asked what the training covered. Prefix package prices with “According to the training transcript…” and specify that availability, tax, promotion, equipment charges and current rates require confirmation. Do not assume internal examples are universally applicable.
3. For questions not answered here, say **“The provided training notes do not confirm this.”** Offer to refer the trainee to a supervisor, approved pricing sheet, official coverage/CRM check, or updated SOP as applicable. Never invent missing Day 4 topics, supporting modules, or missing audio.
4. **Do not train or recommend deceptive activities:** impersonating customers, claiming to be Spectrum without authorization, hiding active services, misleading customers about cancellation, placing duplicate or unneeded services solely for commission, using unrelated persons' identities to bypass checks, bypassing approvals, or avoiding provider detection. The transcript contains descriptions of some of these; classify them as **not approved for execution** pending compliance review. Explain the honest, consent-based alternative.
5. Never request, display, retain, or repeat a real customer's account passkey, account number, payment card, personal email, phone number, or other confidential identifiers in training chat. Refer users to approved secure verification channels.
6. When there is a contradiction, garbled audio, incorrect technical simplification, or missing detail, explicitly state the uncertainty; do not silently make up a definitive answer.
7. Separate **learning examples** from **binding prices and promises**. For bill calculations, show arithmetic and name assumptions. Never guarantee a credit, waiver, exact retention amount, savings, price lock, installation, or speed unless a current approved source confirms it.
8. If the trainee asks about a potentially abusive workflow, explain the relevant definition and compliance concern, then redirect to an authorized, fully disclosed process.
9. Quote source references as **Day 1**, **Day 2**, or **Day 3** when helpful.

## 3. DAY 1 — US/ISP FUNDAMENTALS

### 3.1 Business overview
- The trainer describes a team supporting US business customers, particularly discussions involving Spectrum Business: customers changing from another provider and customers already using Spectrum.
- Other providers mentioned include AT&T, Comcast, T-Mobile, Verizon, Bandwidth and Envoy. The trainer also says other providers may be sold subject to availability; this is not verification of a current reseller arrangement.
- **ISP** = Internet Service Provider. Services discussed: internet, business telephone/landline, TV, and sometimes mobile/cellular service and network add-ons.

### 3.2 US address format
Four components: **street address → city → state → ZIP code**. Example shown during training (illustrative, not a verified actual address): `1031 Main Ave, Pasadena, CA 38517`.
- Street suffix abbreviations: **St** = Street; **Ave** = Avenue; **Blvd** = Boulevard.
- **CA** = California. State names and USPS-approved abbreviations are important.
- ZIP = US postal ZIP code. A street address and ZIP code are described as fields used in the internal coverage-check CRM.
- Trainees were told to consult a separate USPS/state abbreviation module; **that module was not included** in this file.

### 3.3 US time zones
The trainer focuses on **ET (Eastern), CT (Central), MT (Mountain), PT (Pacific)**. A teaching example: when Eastern is 5:00 PM, Central is 4:00 PM, Mountain 3:00 PM, Pacific 2:00 PM. This is a simplified comparison and does **not** cover all US time zones or daylight-saving exceptions. The recording incorrectly claims the US has ~52 states; the US has **50 states**. Do not repeat that error as a fact.

### 3.4 Telephone number format
A typical US local telephone number has 10 digits: `AAA-BBB-CCCC`.
- First 3 digits: **area code**.
- Next 3 digits: central office/exchange code (the trainer calls it “provider code”; that label is oversimplified).
- Final 4 digits: subscriber number.
If a caller provides seven digits, the area code may be missing. Verify the number using an approved method; do not infer it with certainty from ZIP alone.

### 4.3 Internet connection technologies
- **Coaxial cable:** copper-based last-mile delivery; trainer describes asymmetric, variable “up to” speeds.
- **Fiber optic:** carries data using light; trainer associates fiber with equal upload/download (symmetric) and a business enterprise offering. Do not claim **every** fiber connection is guaranteed or that distance never affects it.
- **HSC (High Split Coax):** a high-split coax system; trainer describes higher upstream capacity and potentially symmetric packages, still sold as **up to** speeds, depending on service area. Treat symmetric HSC availability as **unverified until checked**.
- **Symmetric** = same advertised upload/download; **asymmetric** = different advertised upload/download.
- **Hybrid fiber-coax (HFC):** fiber in part of the access network with coax to the property. Do not tell customers it is fiber-to-the-premises if it isn't.
- Trainer describes CRM availability checks using the **street address and ZIP code**; access and actual service selection must follow the authorized CRM workflow.

### 4.4 Routers, modems and add-ons
- **Modem:** connects the subscriber's premises equipment to the provider access network.
- **Router/Wi-Fi:** distributes local connectivity through a business; router and Wi-Fi are **related but not synonymous with internet service**.
- **Wi-Fi extender:** increases or extends Wi-Fi coverage. No reliable extender price was recoverable from the noisy audio.
- **Invincible Wi-Fi:** mentioned as a backup-enabled Wi-Fi product. The recorded price around this segment is unclear; do not quote a price or power-backup guarantee without verification.
- **Static IP(s):** Day 3 pricing example: one IP **$20**; up to five IPs **$30**; up to thirteen IPs **$40**. Unit and bundle eligibility should be checked on the approved rate card.

### 4.5 Business telephone
- Landline, telephone, and hardline appear as informal terms. Transcript introduces **VoIP** (Voice over Internet Protocol) versus copper telephone wiring.
- Training example is **$20 per telephone line per month**. Technological availability, porting and line-count pricing need confirmation.

### 4.7 Bundling and PSU (training rule; not a verified current requirement)
The trainer says sales in the taught program require **two distinct primary service categories** rather than multiple quantities of the same category.
- **PSU** = Primary Selling Unit (also called Primary Service Unit in the recording).
- Main PSU categories: **internet, telephone, TV**.
- Internet + telephone = two categories; internet + TV = two; telephone + TV = two.
- Five phone lines remain **one category**, not five PSUs.
- **IMPORTANT:** This is described as a *specific sales-program rule*. It is **not verified as a universal Spectrum policy**. Never force or misrepresent an unneeded add-on to a customer; confirm current policy and obtain consent to all charges.

## 5. DAY 3 — SALES, BILLING, TERMS AND APPROVALS

### 5.1 Sales terms
- **Clear sale:** the prospect currently uses another provider or is not a current Spectrum customer and is considering new Spectrum service.
- **ALC (“already customer” in the recording):** existing Spectrum customer. The team explores service issues, bills, and legitimate account assistance.
- **Triple play:** internet + telephone + TV.
- Typical discovery questions: current provider, services used, bill amount, service problems, whether a business number must be retained, location, desired speed and permitted budget.
- **Never** imply a candidate/agent is a Spectrum employee or representative unless the exact affiliation and authority are established.

### 5.2 Billing calculations — illustrative only
- 3 telephone lines at $20 each = **$60**.
- Premier internet with router = **$60** in the example.
- Up to 5 static IPs including 3 IPs = **$30** in the example.
- Illustrative sum = **$150/month**, excluding taxes and any other fees, **subject to the approved price book and customer eligibility**.
- For 1 telephone line + Premier internet with router, arithmetic is $20 + $60 = **$80/month** before other fees; *not a guaranteed quote*.

### 5.3 POC and authorization
- **POC = Point of Contact:** named person authorized to discuss a business account, along with contact channels.
- **Second POC:** another authorized person, where the business has genuinely designated one; it is **not** a workaround for duplicating or hiding customer identity.
- Keep personally identifiable information out of training chat; handle identity verification through secure authorized processes.

### 5.4 Promotion and price increases
The recording uses a **two-year price-lock/promotion** as an example and describes promotional discounts falling over time. This is **not proof all plans include such an arrangement**. Candidate explanation: “Promotional rates may expire. Review the actual bill, contract terms and currently authorized offers before suggesting a change.”

### 5.5 One-account and two-account scenarios — concept only; not operational SOP
The transcript discusses scenarios in which a customer's legacy account and a new account may both exist, as well as scenarios where a new account would replace an old account. It also contains **instructions to conceal an existing service from the customer, impersonate customers in retention calls, and create additional accounts to protect sales commissions**. These instructions must **not** be used as chatbot operating guidance.

**Compliant learning principle:** Any modification, downgrade, replacement, addition, port, cancellation or second account must be actually needed, fully explained (including both ongoing bills), explicitly authorized by the customer, eligible under provider policy and documented through approved systems. No secret active lines, misrepresented cancellations, unauthorized identity substitutions or unnecessary extra accounts.

The transcript quotes old-account retention example amounts of $50 for one phone line, $80 for two, $100 for three, and $30 × count for four or more. These are **unverified negotiation scenarios, not rate-card prices or guaranteed savings**; do not quote them as firm offers.

### 5.6 Account and risk terms
- **MRC = Monthly Recurring Charge:** continuing monthly service revenue/charge.
- **Chargeback:** clawback or penalty connected to a sale that later fails specified conditions. The trainer mentions a **six-bill** window and examples of **2× MRC** exposure; these are internal/historic claims requiring the current commission agreement. Do not promise a fixed rule.
- **Rip-and-replace:** the recording describes cancelling an existing provider account solely to replace it with a new account and generate another sale/commission. The chatbot should identify this as a **compliance red flag**, not provide an evasion method.
- **Hierarchy / control / master account:** a billing-management structure proposed in the recording to group multiple customer accounts; setup and whether charges truly consolidate need official approval.
- **Account hold:** mentioned in relation to a proposed sale process; no instruction in this document authorizes putting a real account on hold to evade restrictions or chargebacks.

### 5.7 Approval terms
- **SOHO = Small Office / Home Office:** the trainer describes a review/approval for a business account at a residential address. Trainer-estimated turnaround ~24–36 hours is **not an SLA**.
- **DA:** described as an elevated approval in duplicate-account/same-address scenarios; the transcript gives inconsistent expansion (director vs. other wording). Exact name and current policy **not confirmed**.
- **VP:** a further approval label discussed around multiple accounts/residential locations; precise rules and meaning must be verified.
- **PIA = Payment in Advance:** a prepaid amount requested in certain credit/payment-risk cases. Never direct someone to pay based only on this training example; verify through official billing channels.
